Overview

Belief Pad is a launchpad on Solana. You launch a coin on pump.fun through Belief Pad, and the coin appears on a public board ranked by Belief: a score out of 1,000 that measures how many people hold the coin and how long they have held it.

Most boards rank coins by price or volume, which a few traders can move in minutes. Belief ranks on something slower and more expensive to fake: people choosing to stay.

  • Launch. Create a coin with a locked fee split. Creators keep most of the fees.
  • Belief. A score that updates every five minutes. Holders who stay longer and keep their position count for more. Fake and oversized wallets count for less.
  • Rewards. The most believed coins are paid every hour and every day, in $BELIEFPAD. Half of each payout goes to that coin's holders.

Launching a coin

  1. Connect a wallet.This wallet becomes the creator wallet. It is left out of every Belief count for the coin.
  2. Stake to launch.Stake at least 0.1% of the $BELIEFPAD supply (1,000,000 $BELIEFPAD). This keeps spam launches off the board.
  3. Describe the coin.Name, ticker, image and a short description. We pin the image and metadata ourselves so they don't depend on a public gateway.
  4. Lock the fee split.The standard split is 70% to you and 30% to the Belief Pad treasury. Creators who stake 1% of supply or more launch with 80/20. The split is set once, at launch, and nobody can change it afterward, including us.
  5. Launch on pump.fun.Creating the coin and locking the split happen in one flow, so a coin is never left unlocked. An optional first buy is a second approval in your wallet.
  6. We check the lock.Before listing, Belief Pad reads the coin's fee-sharing setup on-chain. If the split isn't the one you chose, or can still be changed, the coin is not listed. If a coin was created but not locked, you can retry the setup.
  7. Warm up.New coins sit under "Warming up" until they are 15 minutes old and have 5 qualifying holders. Then they get a rank.

Creator fees collect in a per-coin vault until they are distributed. Belief Pad runs the distribution on a schedule, and anyone can trigger it.

The Belief score

Belief is a number from 0 to 1,000. It is built in two steps: weigh every wallet, then combine the wallets into one score for the coin.

Step 1: weigh each wallet

A wallet's weight is three things multiplied together.

wallet weight = time × kept × size

Time is how long the wallet has held. It rises quickly in the first hours and reaches full weight at 24 hours.

time = 0.85 × (1 − e^(−hours ÷ 1.5)) + 0.15 × min(hours ÷ 24, 1)

The first part of the formula does most of the work, so a new holder sees their effect within the hour. The second part is a slow ramp that rewards the holders who make it to a full day.

Kept is the wallet's current balance divided by the largest balance it has reached in this holding streak. A wallet that never sold is at 100%. Sell half and it is at 50%. Sell everything and the streak ends: if the wallet buys again, its clock starts from zero.

Size depends on the USD value of the position. Tiers stop at 1.75×, so a very large wallet counts for less than two small ones.

For example, a wallet holding $120 for 3 hours that has not sold has a weight of 0.754 × 1 × 1.25 = 0.94. If it sells half, the weight is 0.754 × 0.5 × 1.25 = 0.47.

Step 2: combine the wallets

belief points (B) = sum of every wallet's weight reach = log10(1 + B ÷ 10) ÷ log10(1 + 1,500) capped at 1 spread = 1 − (top-10 share − 20%) ÷ 80% kept between 0.40 and 1 Belief = 1,000 × reach × spread

Reach turns belief points into a number between 0 and 1 on a log scale. Going from 100 holders to 1,000 matters roughly as much as going from 1,000 to 10,000, so small coins can climb and large coins can't run away. A coin reaches the cap at 15,000 belief points.

Spread penalizes concentration. If the ten largest wallets hold 20% of the supply or less, there is no penalty. At 60% the spread is 0.50. It never drops below 0.40, so a new coin with concentrated early holders is held back, not wiped out.

Rankings

  • Snapshots. Belief is calculated every 5 minutes. The score on the board is the average of the last three snapshots (15 minutes), so the board moves without flickering.
  • Who ranks. A coin ranks once it is at least 15 minutes old and has 5 qualifying holders. Until then it shows under "Warming up" with its progress.
  • Sorting. The board sorts by Belief by default. "Rising" sorts by the biggest 15-minute change and "Newest" by launch time.
  • Ties. Equal scores are ordered by more belief points, then by longer typical hold time.
  • Rings. Each score has a ring that fills as it grows. A coin earns Lit at 250, Diamond at 500 and Crown at 800.
  • Nothing hidden. Select any coin to see the numbers behind its score: holders, hold time, position kept, size tier and spread.
  • Left out of every count. The creator wallet, the bonding curve and liquidity pool, known exchange wallets, and any wallet funded by the creator.

Fair play

Any ranking that pays out money will be attacked. These rules make faking Belief expensive.

  • Dust filter. Wallets holding under $10 do not count.
  • Wallet clusters. Wallets funded from the same source are treated as a cluster, and a cluster of n wallets counts as √n wallets. For example, 500 separate holders who kept everything for six hours would give a coin a Belief of 519. The same 500 wallets from one funder count as 22.4 wallets and give 148. (Both assume the smallest size tier and no spread penalty.)
  • Transfers don't carry time. Tokens received by transfer start the receiving wallet's clock at zero.
  • Payouts use the hour's average. Hourly payouts rank coins by their average Belief across the hour, not by one moment, so a last-minute spike earns nothing.
  • Reward wait. A wallet must have held for 30 minutes before it shares in a coin's holder reward.
  • Verified fee lock. Only coins whose fee split is verified on-chain are listed.

No score is impossible to game. Belief makes it costly, and the rules above are expected to change as we see how people try.

  • Creator and holders. Each coin's payout is split 50% to its creator and 50% to its eligible holders, in proportion to each wallet's weight. Holders are paid for holding, which also keeps the score honest.
  • Claiming. Rewards build up and are claimed in one transaction, so small payouts aren't lost to network fees.
  • Variable. Pool sizes depend on trading fees and change every hour. Rewards are not guaranteed.

Burns

Buybacks run every 10 minutes in small, randomized amounts, so the timing can't be predicted. Once an hour there is a Burn Hour with an extra burn. A live ticker on the site shows how much has been burned in the last hour.

What $BELIEFPAD is used for

  • Stake to launch. 0.1% of supply to list a coin.
  • Lower fee share. Creators who stake 1% launch with an 80/20 split instead of 70/30.
  • Spotlight. Burn $BELIEFPAD to pin a coin to the top of the home page for 24 hours.
  • Staker rewards. 6% of all fees, paid daily to stakers, plus an early look at rising coins.
  • Rewards currency. Hourly and daily payouts are in $BELIEFPAD, so every payout is also a purchase.

The loop: more launches bring more fees, more fees fund more burns and rewards, and a stronger $BELIEFPAD gives creators more reason to launch here.
